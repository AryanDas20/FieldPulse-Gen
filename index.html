CALCULATION STUDIO / 04

Farm tools library.

Practical calculators for planning and record preparation. Each tool explains what it estimates, what you must enter, and how to interpret the result.

15 FIELD TOOLS
⌕
💧TOOL 01

Water Requirement Estimator

Convert a field area and a net water depth into an estimated volume. Application efficiency adjusts the gross amount that may need to be delivered.

What this tool means

One millimetre of water over one hectare equals 10,000 litres. This estimate does not decide the correct depth; enter a depth from a crop-water plan or local advice.

ESTIMATE—

Enter field assumptions to calculate gross volume.

ϟTOOL 02

Pump Runtime & Energy Cost

Estimate how long a pump would run for a target volume, then estimate energy use and electricity cost from entered power and tariff.

What this tool means

Runtime = volume ÷ flow. Energy = power in kilowatts × hours. Use measured flow and the pump's electrical input rating for a better estimate.

ESTIMATE—

Results depend on actual pump flow and power draw.

⌁TOOL 03

Soil Sensor Calibration Helper

Map an analogue sensor value between your own dry and wet reference points into a simple 0–100 index.

What this tool means

Different probes and soil conditions behave differently. A normalized index is not the same as a laboratory volumetric water-content reading.

NORMALIZED INDEX—

Use reference measurements from your own probe.

▤TOOL 04

Multi-Zone Schedule Planner

Estimate total sequential run time and water volume when zones share one pump and operate one after another.

What this tool means

This assumes each zone has the same duration and active-zone flow. It does not account for pressure variation, soak time or switching delays.

SEQUENTIAL PLAN—

Enter the number of zones and operating assumptions.

◷TOOL 05

Root-Zone Irrigation Interval

Estimate a theoretical water buffer in the root zone and divide it by a daily crop-water-use value entered by you.

Formula and caution

Available water depth = (field capacity − wilting point) × root depth. Readily available water = available water × allowed depletion. Interval = readily available water ÷ daily use. Soil values and depletion fraction require local measurement and agronomic guidance.

THEORETICAL ESTIMATE—

Use representative soil data and a locally suitable crop-water value.

☂TOOL 06

Rainwater Harvest Potential

Estimate a gross collection volume from rainfall, catchment area and a runoff coefficient that you choose.

Formula and caution

Potential litres = rainfall in mm × catchment area in m² × runoff coefficient. Actual collection will be lower because of first flush, leakage, overflow, filtration and storage limits.

GROSS POTENTIAL—

Gross collection is not guaranteed usable supply.

❋TOOL 07

Seed Quantity Planner

Adjust a seed-rate figure to account for germination and physical purity, then scale it to your field area.

Formula and caution

Adjusted seed kg/ha = entered target seed rate ÷ (germination fraction × purity fraction). Use the target rate and seed-lot test results recommended for your crop and variety.

SEED LOT ESTIMATE—

Enter tested germination and purity percentages.

✿TOOL 08

Fertilizer Product Calculator

Convert a nutrient quantity from a soil-test or agronomist plan into a product mass using the nutrient percentage printed on the bag.

Formula and caution

Product kg = target nutrient kg ÷ (label percentage ÷ 100). This only converts units; it does not recommend a fertilizer rate, nutrient balance or application timing.

PRODUCT MASS—

Use the nutrient percentage shown on your product label.

ϟTOOL 09

Pump Hydraulic Power Estimator

Estimate idealised hydraulic input power from flow, total head and pump efficiency. It is a first-pass calculation, not pump selection.

Formula and caution

Hydraulic power = water density × gravity × flow in m³/s × head. Divide by pump efficiency to estimate shaft input. Pipe losses, motor efficiency, start current and pump curves matter in real design.

ENGINEERING ESTIMATE—

Use a pump curve and qualified design review for actual equipment.

⚗TOOL 10

Label-Based Spray Mix Calculator

Multiply a rate taken directly from a product label by tank volume. The tool does not suggest chemicals, products, rates or whether spraying is appropriate.

Use safely

Follow the exact local product label and law, PPE, buffer zones, re-entry and pre-harvest intervals. Never use a rate from another crop or formulation. If the label is unclear, ask a licensed local adviser.

LABEL ARITHMETIC ONLY—

Copy the rate from the exact product label.

⌕TOOL 11

Crop Scouting Incidence Tracker

Calculate the percentage of inspected plants that show a chosen symptom and create a consistent scouting record.

What this tool means

Incidence = affected plants ÷ inspected plants × 100. This is a monitoring metric—not an economic threshold or treatment trigger.

SCOUTING METRIC—

Repeat the same sampling method when comparing dates.

₹TOOL 12

Farm Investment Payback

Estimate simple payback from costs and benefits that you enter. Use measured savings only; include repair, labour and financing costs in a real business case.

Formula and caution

Net annual benefit = annual benefit − annual running cost. Simple payback = initial investment ÷ net annual benefit, only when that benefit is positive. This omits discounting and risk.

FINANCIAL SCENARIO—

Use comparable, documented farm costs and benefits.

∿TOOL 13

Daily Crop Water Balance

Estimate a daily irrigation deficit by subtracting effective rain and a soil-water contribution from crop water use entered by you.

Formula and caution

Net deficit = max(0, crop water use - effective rain - soil contribution). Gross depth = net deficit / application efficiency. Volume in litres = gross depth in mm x field area in square metres. This is a simple balance, not a full evapotranspiration model.

DAILY WATER BALANCE—

Use measured or locally validated crop-water values.

◫TOOL 14

Irrigation Uniformity Checker

Compare four catch-container measurements to see how evenly water was distributed during a simple field test.

Formula and caution

For four samples, the lower-quarter average is the smallest sample. Lower-quarter distribution uniformity = lowest sample / overall average x 100. Use more samples for a proper field evaluation and follow an accepted test method.

LOWER-QUARTER UNIFORMITY—

Use equal containers and a consistent test duration.

▥TOOL 15

Harvest Storage Space Estimator

Estimate storage volume from a harvest mass, bulk density and the portion of storage volume you plan to fill.

Formula and caution

Required volume = harvest mass in kg / bulk density in kg per cubic metre / fill fraction. Bulk density varies by crop, moisture, variety and handling; use a measured or reliable crop-specific value.

STORAGE VOLUME ESTIMATE—

Check actual crop density, ventilation, access and storage specifications.

ⓘ
Calculation is not agronomic advice.

All formulas use the numbers you enter. Crop rates, nutrient requirements, soil parameters, irrigation intervals, product labels and equipment specifications must come from reliable measurements and local expert guidance.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Clear answers, no hidden assumptions.

Does FieldPulse connect to real sensors, weather or a pump?

No. The current version is a standalone front-end website. It uses simulated or manually entered inputs and does not connect to live hardware, weather services or a farm database.

Are the AI tools trained AI models?

No. They are transparent local rule helpers, checklists and phrase generators. A real AI integration would need a model/API, secure data handling, testing and a clear human-review process.

Are crop thresholds and fertilizer figures recommendations?

No. Crop thresholds are illustrative. Fertilizer and irrigation calculators only convert values you supply. Use soil tests, product labels and current local agronomy guidance.

Where are field records stored?

In the current browser's local storage. Export JSON or CSV backups. The entries are not automatically sent to a server or shared with another device.

Can I connect this website to an ESP32 and a pump?

The website alone is not a safe pump controller. A real project needs a separate embedded controller, correctly rated isolated hardware, dry-run and overcurrent protection, a safe default state and supervised electrical testing. Do not rely on a browser page for essential safety control.
